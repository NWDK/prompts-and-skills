# Decisions: triangulate load-bearing facts

## This replaced a heavier piece

The queued version was a research method: two model tracks reconciled
by evidence lineage, 338 lines. Real, but it answers a
question most people don't have. What they ask about is smaller: the
assistant grabbed a number and everything got built on it. This is that
rule alone; the two-track method is one paragraph at the end.

## The trigger is the hard part, not the action

"Get a second source" is advice everyone agrees with and nobody applies:
applied to everything, it's paralysing. The rule needs a threshold: does
future work rest on this being right?

## "Tell me if you can't" does more work than it looks

Without it, asking for two sources reliably produces two, whether or
not two exist. It turns retrieval into a question with a real negative
answer.

## Checking the source is intact came from a separate failure

Added after a research export read complete while its numbers were
lost in translation: a chart that doesn't survive conversion, leaving
prose describing a figure gone.

## The graduated response, rather than a gate

Early drafts stopped at "corroborate it or don't use it." Wrong:
sometimes there's genuinely one source and work still has to happen. Use
it, mark it, keep it soft.

## The worked example is sanitised, and checked

Real: a ratio pulled mid-trend from a dashboard, published without the
company, product, platform or figures. The gap is described as "a small
fraction", not a rounded multiple, because a piece about unchecked numbers
can't round one for effect.
