# Decisions: the logo swap test

## The name was arguing with the piece

This was drafted as "AI Design Slop Audit". The README's own thesis is that
whether a machine made it is mostly the wrong question, so the name
contradicted the argument in its first three words.

Named for the test instead. It is the line people repeat back to me, it
survives model drift because it contains no claim about any particular
tool, and it points at the thing that actually matters: an interface that
belongs to nobody.

## The signal register stayed home

The internal version carries a reference table of current defaults, stamped
July 2026, plus rendered comparison plates.

That table is the operational half and it is the half that rots. Publishing
it would mean committing to a maintenance model I do not have, and a stale
tell-list is worse than none: it fails in the confident direction, flagging
things that stopped being signals and missing what replaced them.

Everything published here is drift-proof by construction. The logo swap
test, the classification, genre controls, blind-first, and the evaluator
blind spot all work regardless of which defaults are current this quarter.

## The numeric score was built, then killed

The first version scored out of twenty. It went because a number launders a
judgement call into something that looks measured, and the number survives
downstream long after the reasoning that produced it is gone. Someone
quotes "14/20" in a meeting; nobody quotes the four findings underneath it.

Four categorical verdicts instead, including **Unscored**, which exists so
"I could not see enough to say" has somewhere to go that isn't a low score.

## Genre controls are the part that makes it an audit

Without them this is a banned-motif list with extra steps, and banned lists
fail in both directions. They punish editorial for using serifs and
developer tools for being dark, and they clear a thoroughly generic
interface assembled from unfashionable parts.

The design-system exclusion is the sharpest case: a specimen page is
supposed to look like its own defaults, so running provenance findings
against one produces a confident wrong answer every time.

## Blind-first is a real gate, not a nicety

Once you know a model produced something, you will find model tells, and
you cannot separate that from having seen them. The blind pass has to come
before the source read or it may as well not happen.

Same principle as the independent QA gate in `three-gates-and-a-lock`: the
value is in what the observer does not yet know.

## Two cases, called two cases

The own-family blind spot is reproduced twice, in both directions, and
that is exactly how it is described. It is not published as a law, a rate,
or a percentage.

That matters more here than in most pieces, because the finding is the kind
that would be very easy to inflate into "models systematically under-rate
their own family by N%". I have two observations. The guidance they support
is "drop your confidence and mark it provisional", which is all two
observations can carry.
