# Methods

Write-ups of how something was done, where the value is the reasoning
rather than a file you install.

A method isn't a skill or a template. There's nothing to copy into a
`skills/` folder and nothing to paste into a settings box. You read it,
and either it changes how you do the thing or it doesn't.

Most of these exist because a system I built turned out to be less
transferable than what I learned building it. The pipeline was shaped by my
projects, my filing, my tools. The findings underneath it weren't.

## Adding one

- **Lead with what you found, not what you built.** If the interesting part
  is your architecture, it's probably a skill. If the interesting part is a
  thing that turned out to be true, it's a method.
- **Say how you know.** Measured, counted, or noticed over time are three
  different claims. Write which one it is. "Noticed over two years" is
  honest and useful; the same thing dressed as a statistic is neither.
- **Scope the finding to what you actually saw.** A true statement about
  one tool, written as a statement about all of them, is how a document
  becomes false without anyone editing it.
- **No configuration.** A method that needs setting up has become a skill.
